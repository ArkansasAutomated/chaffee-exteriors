## Quick answer

Before you buy gutter guards from anyone — a national franchise system or a local installer — ask three questions: What's the total price per foot, in writing, before work starts? Do I get to keep my existing gutters? And what's covered if something clogs or fails? Get all three in writing and you'll avoid the industry's worst surprises.

## The two ways gutter guards get sold

In Fort Smith, gutter guards are sold two ways.

**The national way:** a sales representative comes to your home, walks the roofline with you, and sits down at your kitchen table for a presentation that can run two to three hours. The price is often presented as a special offer contingent on signing that day. Quotes in this model commonly run $15 to $45 per linear foot, and it's not unusual for a whole-house quote to reach several thousand dollars.

**The local way:** someone measures your gutters, prices the job by the foot, and hands you the number in writing — usually without any presentation at all. That's the model we use: $12 per linear foot for one story, $14 for two, $16 for three, with most installations running $599–$1,999.

Neither approach is automatically right or wrong for every homeowner. But they produce very different buying experiences, and the questions below are designed to make either one answerable on paper.

## Question 1: "What's my total price per foot — in writing, before you start?"

This is the single most important question in gutter-guard shopping, because it's where the industry's pricing opacity lives.

A reputable installer can tell you, before any work starts:

- The price per linear foot for your home's story height
- The total linear footage they measured
- The all-in total, including materials, labor, and any old-gutter prep

If the answer is "we'll know after we custom-quote your home," or the number only appears after a long in-home presentation, you're being sold rather than quoted. A written, per-foot price protects you from the most common bait-and-switch: a low advertised rate that somehow multiplies once the crew is on site.

## Question 2: "Do I get to keep my existing gutters?"

Here's an industry secret worth knowing: **many homes don't need new gutters to get guards.** If your existing gutters are structurally sound — good hangers, no rot behind the fascia, correct sizing — a micro-mesh guard system can be fitted directly on top of them.

Some national systems bundle a complete gutter replacement into the guard installation, even when the existing gutters have years of life left. That's not inherently dishonest — some homes genuinely need new gutters — but it's a major cost difference, and the homeowner should be the one deciding based on the actual condition of their gutters, not on a sales script.

Ask for photos of your existing gutters and an honest assessment: which sections are fine, which need repair, which (if any) need replacement. "They're all fine, we'll guard what you have" should be an acceptable answer from any installer.

## Question 3: "What happens if something clogs or fails?"

No gutter guard is maintenance-free, and no guard works perfectly on every roof in every storm. The honest questions are:

- What's the warranty on materials, and what does it actually cover — clogging, sagging, finish failure?
- Who services the system if a section fails — and is that service free, discounted, or full price?
- What maintenance does the manufacturer or installer recommend, and how often?

Be wary of "you'll never clean your gutters again" claims. Quality micro-mesh dramatically reduces cleaning frequency, but a heavily wooded lot may still need an occasional rinse or touch-up. An installer who says that out loud is telling you the truth.

## Two red flags worth naming

Beyond the three questions, two patterns should slow any homeowner down:

1. **Today-only pricing.** A quote that expires when the salesperson leaves the driveway is a pressure tactic, not a price. Real prices survive a weekend of comparison shopping. (We don't use time-limited discounts — the written quote is the price.)

2. **Refusal to leave the quote behind.** Any installer confident in their number will leave it with you. If the paperwork "has to be signed now," that's not an estimate — it's a trap.

## FAQ

**How much do gutter guards cost in Fort Smith?**
Installed prices locally follow national ranges of roughly $6–$13 per linear foot for standard installs, with premium branded systems running higher. Our published rates are $12–$16 per foot depending on story height.

**Are gutter guards worth it in Arkansas?**
On tree-covered lots, usually yes — they cut cleaning frequency dramatically. On open lots with minimal canopy, a twice-yearly cleaning may cost less over time.

**Can guards go on old gutters?**
Often, yes — if hangers are sound and the fascia behind them is solid. A reputable installer will inspect and tell you honestly which sections qualify.

**Do gutter guards eliminate cleaning forever?**
No system does. Quality micro-mesh reduces maintenance to an occasional surface rinse on most homes.
