## Quick answer

Most Fort Smith homes need gutter cleaning **twice a year: late spring after the oak tassels drop, and late fall after the leaves come down**. Pine-covered lots may need three or four cleanings. If you do only one, make it late fall — winter clogs set up foundation and ice problems by spring.

## Why River Valley timing is different

Gutter advice on the internet is written for everywhere, which usually means the Northeast's big single leaf-drop. The Arkansas River Valley runs on a different clock. Fort Smith gets about 47 inches of rain a year, spread fairly evenly across all four seasons, with heavy spring storms and a long growing season. What that means for your gutters:

- **Debris arrives in waves, not one dump.** Oak catkins and tassels in April and May, seed pods in early summer, leaves from October through December, and pine needles all year. There's no single "clean it and forget it" moment.
- **Rain doesn't wait for you.** A cleaning schedule that assumes a dry month between leaf drop and the first storm doesn't work here. November rain on top of fresh leaf drop is when the worst clogs happen.
- **Pine needles never stop.** If you have loblolly or shortleaf pines over your roofline — common in Fianna Hills, Southside, and around Greenwood — needles accumulate every month of the year and weave themselves into dense mats that block downspout elbows.

## The River Valley cleaning calendar

**Late April – May (spring cleaning).** The first heavy debris wave of the year: oak tassels, flowers, seeds, and the leftovers of winter. Spring cleaning matters less for leaves and more for **downspout elbows** — spring debris is stringy and packs tight exactly where the pipe bends. This cleaning also preps the system for our stormiest months: May and June are typically among Fort Smith's wettest, per National Weather Service climate data.

**Late October – December (fall cleaning).** The essential one. Fall leaves are the volume event of the year, and a gutter that goes into winter clogged stays clogged through freeze-thaw cycles, winter rain, and early-spring storms. Clogged gutters in January don't just overflow — they hold standing water that freezes, expands, and strains every hanger and seam on the house.

**As-needed cleanings (the pine factor).** If your home sits under pine canopy, add a mid-summer check. Pine-needle mats are the most common cause of the "one overflowing corner" problem you'll often find across Fort Smith and Van Buren.

## Warning signs you can't wait for the calendar

Schedule a cleaning as soon as you spot any of these:

- **Water spilling over gutter edges** during rain — a clog, not a capacity problem.
- **One overflowing corner** — almost always a packed downspout elbow.
- **Plants growing from the gutter** — enough soil has accumulated for seeds to germinate; you're months past due.
- **Streaks or stains on siding** below gutter lines — chronic overflow.
- **Gutters pulling away or sagging** — weight from packed debris and standing water.
- **Mosquitoes around the roofline in dry weather** — they're breeding in standing water in the gutter channel.

**What happens if I never clean my gutters?**
